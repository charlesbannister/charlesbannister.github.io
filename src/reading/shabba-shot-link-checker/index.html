Broken link checker

November 20, 2026

Imagine your ecommerce site moves to a new collection structure on Tuesday. The team tested the homepage and checkout. Somewhere in Google Ads, a sitelink still points to a removed collection. Find which paid destinations fail or redirect and decide what needs attention first.

Who needs it, and when?

A site migration gives an agency lead a reason to run this today. The current options include a website crawler, a link-checking script and clicking through ads by hand. The difference to show is that a paid URL comes with spend and the Google Ads items that use it.

Separate the person finding the problem from the person fixing it. A PPC manager may update the ads, while a site developer may need to repair an intended redirect. The result can give both people the same URL and a clear account of what happened.

Draft page copy

After the site goes live, where do your ads go?

Check where your Google Ads links really go after a site change. Find failed destinations and unexpected redirects, see which ads and assets use them, and review replacement URLs in spend order.

What to show in a demo

  1. Use sample data for a removed collection page and a working redirect to its proper replacement.
  2. Trace the failed URL to a sitelink and an enabled ad.
  3. Open the proposed replacement and verify the destination before showing its affected placements.

Content angles

The post-migration check paid media teams own

When a redirect is worth fixing
Show a valid redirect that preserves the offer and one that takes a shopper somewhere unrelated.

Questions a buyer might ask

Do redirects always need replacing?
Check the final page against the ad’s promise. A redirect to the right replacement can be intentional.

A checker can encounter temporary errors or automated-request blocks. Confirm a finding before changing URLs in Google Ads.
