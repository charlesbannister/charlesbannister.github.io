Account trends

November 20, 2026

Imagine an agency lead with six Google Ads accounts to review and a call in half an hour. One account is down on the previous week but in line with last year. Another is losing more Search impression share to rank. The job is to pick where to spend the next hour.

Who needs it, and when?

Start with the person who has to do a round of checks across clients. A single-account advertiser can still use the splits, but the clearest value is the Monday decision across several accounts. One row per account matters because a strong week in one client's account should never erase a problem in another's.

Their alternative is opening separate accounts and lining up reports. Show where the shared timeline helps the lead make a call, then open one account and choose a split like device or channel. The payoff is a defensible order for the morning and a concrete task someone can pick up.

What the Shot does for them

Multiple accounts in one view, with a row for each
Compare the accounts on the same timeline and keep each client’s performance in its own context.
Weekly or monthly change alongside the same period last year
Tell when the change started and see whether the same part of the year looked similar last time.
Impression share, loss to budget or rank and change markers
Choose what to check next and see where reported budget, bid strategy, target or status changes happened.

Draft page copy

Which client account needs you first on Monday?

Open the accounts you look after on one timeline. Compare each week or month with the same period last year, spot which accounts are losing Search impression share to budget or rank, and open the one that needs a closer look. Start your review with a shortlist.

What to show in a demo

  1. Choose three illustrative accounts with different weekly patterns and open them together.
  2. Use last year as context for one account and show a sustained rise in loss to rank on another.
  3. Open the second account, split by device, and inspect a period marker. Explain what you would check next.

Content angles

My Monday Google Ads triage
Film one decision across three accounts. Lead with this because it shows the work the Shot replaces and ends with a task someone can act on.
Budget or rank?
Use the impression-share chart as the starting point for two different investigations. Show what a team member should look at in each case.

Questions a buyer might ask

Does it combine performance across my clients?
Each selected account keeps its own row.
What does a dot above a period mean?
There were tracked changes during that period. Hover to see the changes and use the timing to guide your investigation.

Year-over-year comparisons and change markers add context. Check the account and business history before assigning a cause.
