Negative keyword conflicts

Labs · November 20, 2026

Imagine you launch a repair service and add its search keywords. An old shared list still excludes a word that mattered when you sold equipment only. Before you raise bids, find out whether an exclusion you already have can block what you now want to advertise.

Who needs it, and when?

Lead with an account manager launching a new offer or untangling a mature account. Their current method is to check negative lists by hand or investigate one triggering query at a time. A result that connects an exclusion to the thing it may block gives them somewhere specific to look.

The buyer gets a decision about a particular rule. A shared negative can protect other campaigns for good reasons. Showing a conflict you choose to keep makes the explanation more credible.

What the Shot does for them

Conflicts linked to a keyword, Performance Max search theme or product
See what you intended to advertise alongside the negative that may be in its way.
Campaign, ad group and shared-list context
Work out where a removal would have consequences before deciding what to change.
Strict and wider matching options
Start with clear matches and investigate possible word forms or typos separately.
Spend context where reported and selectable removals
Prioritise what matters to the account and review the exact negatives you might remove.

Draft page copy

Is an old negative keyword blocking your new offer?

Find the negative keyword that may be blocking a keyword, product or Performance Max search theme you meant to advertise. See where the exclusion lives and what it affects, then choose which removals to review.

What to show in a demo

  1. Use a sample account with a new repair keyword and a shared negative phrase containing “repair”.
  2. Show the conflict, then inspect the shared list before deciding whether removing the negative makes sense.
  3. Show the review step with a selected removal and leave the sample change unapplied.

Content angles

Before you raise the bid, check the exclusions
Lead with a launch checklist item and show how to trace one conflict.
One shared list, two very different jobs
Show why the same negative can be useful for one campaign and get in another campaign’s way.

Questions a buyer might ask

Does finding a conflict mean the negative should go?
Review the rule’s purpose and the campaigns it affects before choosing a removal.
Can it remove negatives?
Select the exact negatives to remove and review the change before confirming.

Use a conflict to identify which rule to review. The traffic and revenue impact of removing it depends on what happens after the change.
