Search term n-grams

Labs · November 20, 2026

Picture a PPC manager looking after a roofing company. “Roofer jobs near me”, “roofing jobs with training” and “commercial roofer jobs” sit far apart in the search term report. Grouped together, they show how much of the budget reached people looking for a job.

Who needs it, and when?

Write first for the person who already reviews search terms and knows how easy it is to miss a pattern halfway down an export. Their alternatives are scanning for variants by hand or building a repeatable spreadsheet that splits queries and rolls up the metrics. Show the phrase table in the first demo. The value should be obvious as soon as one phrase brings several scattered searches into view.

Use a costly theme to earn attention. Then show the other reason to use it. A phrase such as “emergency repair” might show up across promising searches and deserve its own landing page test. The decision is what to investigate or test next. Search term tamer is the natural follow-up when the task becomes reviewing potential exclusions.

What the Shot does for them

Words and phrases from one to four words
See the reported activity associated with an expression even when each full query appears only a handful of times.
Search and Performance Max views
See whether a pattern is concentrated in one campaign type before deciding where to look further.
Impressions, clicks, conversion and cost metrics where available
Give a recurring theme enough context to judge whether it deserves a closer look.

Draft page copy

How much did you spend on searches that say “jobs”?

Find the words that keep appearing in the searches you pay for. Search term n-grams groups reported Search and Performance Max queries by the words and phrases they share. See what those groups cost and what they brought in, then choose the themes worth a closer look.

What to show in a demo

  1. Use an illustrative roofing account and show three separate searches with the word “jobs”.
  2. Open the one-word report, show their combined activity under “jobs”, then compare Search with Performance Max.
  3. Use the original queries to judge intent. Finish by showing a second phrase that suggests a landing page test.

Content angles

One word, a dozen tiny rows
Record the jump from disconnected queries to a single phrase. Lead with this because the viewer can see exactly where manual review breaks down.
Which words belong on your next landing page?
Show a phrase appearing across relevant converting queries. Explain how you would verify the pattern before writing the page.

Questions a buyer might ask

What’s an n-gram?
A consecutive group of words. “Roof repair” is a two-word n-gram inside “emergency roof repair near me”.
Can I add the costs from several phrase rows?
The same query can contribute to several phrases. Use the phrase rows individually and take the full account total from Google Ads.

The Shot reads reported terms. Treat phrase metrics as evidence for review and use the source queries to decide whether a phrase belongs in a negative list.
